# Ambiance: the ambient tier

**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Kind:** design record. Mark's rulings on ambiance, the examples he gave, the
stack precedents they cite, and how they sit in Mere's existing session and
memory design. No code.
**Status (2026-09-23):** recorded; every question raised while writing it was
ruled the same day (§9).

The rulings in §2 are Mark's. Quoted text is verbatim. Several rulings were
choices Mark made among options offered to him, and the text says so where it
reports them. Sections marked *analysis* are the assistant's reading of the
rulings; they are not ruled. This document is the stack's named home for the
concept ([DOC_POLICY](../../DOC_POLICY.md) §2). Isocosm's wing design record
keeps its simulation-specific rulings and is cited here, not copied.

## 1. The idea

Mark, 2026-09-23:

> when there is an engine that can generate data or inference, then there are
> gonna be relations between the data the user browses and the rest of the
> domain. those relations, not in focus, are the ambient tier. for any of these
> apps, but this is a very obvious concept if you ask me. consider browsing
> wikipedia. as you go from article to article, all the unclicked links?
> that's the ambient tier.

Mark, 2026-09-20 (Isocosm, ruling 70):

> the background of information generated by an engine and localized to a
> particular address/place/scene... the use in woodshed is a great example of
> how I think about it conceptually. I see it as a characteristic of mere, and
> a wonderful sign for an app when ambiance can be created and used.

Three properties follow from those two statements:

- **It needs an engine.** Ambiance exists where something can generate data or
  inference over a domain: music theory over a chord catalog, a simulation
  over a world, correspondences over a deck, an ephemeris over the sky.
- **It is localized.** It is the background around one address, place, or
  scene, not the whole domain at once. Move the focus and the ambiance moves
  with it.
- **It is out of focus by definition.** Engage part of it and that part is
  foreground; keep it and it is no longer ambient.

The terms, as ruled on 2026-09-23 (§2, §4):
- **Ambiance** is ambient data: what an engine generates in the background of
  a focus.
- **The ambient tier** is the bottom level of the *keeping* axis, below Mere's
  three levels of memory.
- **Attention** is the other axis, and it is independent of keeping.
- **Sessions and codicils.** A mere lives as sessions. Eidetic archives them as
  codicils, and a session can be opened again from a codicil.

## 2. Rulings

Oldest first. The 2026-09-20 rulings are recorded in
`repos/isometry/mesocosm/design_docs/2026-09-18_wing_design_plan.md`; the
2026-09-23 rulings were given in a Cleromancy design session and are recorded
here first.

**What is of note persists (Isocosm ruling 69, 2026-09-20).** "I feel like if
something of note happens there, then it persists; otherwise it can be
regenerated from the same basic facts." And: "Like, a tier between primordial
sim soup things generate from and legendary things. Just stuff of note".

**The soup is the ambient tier, and returning to it is garbage collection
(Isocosm ruling 70, 2026-09-20).** "Ah, the soup, it is equivalent to the
ambient tier, the background of information generated by an engine and
localized to a particular address/place/scene... the use in woodshed is a
great example of how I think about it conceptually. I see it as a
characteristic of mere, and a wonderful sign for an app when ambiance can be
created and used. Things can indeed return to the soup by being made
irrelevant to the player in some manner; that's garbage collection, right? So
the criteria for invalidation is crucial..."

**The roots of collection are care (Isocosm ruling 71, 2026-09-20, and
reading D11, accepted 2026-09-21).** In summary: the roots are the entities
players have selected or created and care about; examination counts as play,
so when nobody plays, whoever is examining is the root; collection is graded,
down to just the event. D11 gives the criteria the shape of roots and
reachability, with the safety test "collect only what nothing reachable from a
player could tell apart from its regenerated self."

**The definition, and several scenes (2026-09-23).** "this absolutely exists
in woodshed. when there is an engine that can generate data or inference, then
there are gonna be relations between the data the user browses and the rest of
the domain. those relations, not in focus, are the ambient tier. for any of
these apps, but this is a very obvious concept if you ask me. consider browsing
wikipedia. as you go from article to article, all the unclicked links? that's
the ambient tier. this obviously directly applies to turnstone. when you search
for a chord in woodshed, what's the related chords in the progression in focus?
how about other progressions? what are the intervals from that chord's root?
what intervalic relations exist? how would you represent them in different
places on the neck? ambient tier. you see why it's important to have multiple
scenes to represent this with, given how different each domain's data relations
can be. lot of latitude, though the force directed node graph is a good default
for a reason."

**Apps keep their session data in a mere, and leverage its ambiance
(2026-09-23).** "this does, once again, sound like a good use for the ambient
tier. As this and all of these apps should use a mere for managing the session
data layer; not to put cards and spreads on a canvas but to keep track of your
readings and notes and staged data. Like knot. Like woodshed. Like turnstone.
Etc. happy to entertain recontextualizations on the idea that are useful
improvements for the stack, but it just seems quite obvious to leverage the
ambient data".

**One mere per data domain, reachable by every app (2026-09-23).** "each data
domain deserves its own mere. that isn't the same as every app getting its own
mere, like two browsers should share a mere if you ask me, but all apps should
be able to access any of an identity's meres by default (at least exceptions
should have a really good reason). the utility here is data portability and
sharing data between apps, like dropbox or google drive or onedrive or icloud
but your own data, your own hardware's storage, your compute mesh. your
personal datalake! your mere. if i were gonna give a term to "data domain," it
would be reservoir or vault. this is the dream of the stack, enabled by the
power of obeying w3c standards and creating apps out of that framework."

Applied to Cleromancy the same day: "Divination, journaling, rng. That's this
bucket. Neighboring: knot (notes, seeds, prompts), redshank (notes), isocosm
(seeds)."

**Be honest about generation (2026-09-23).** "It's necessary to be honest when
things are randomly or otherwise generated, and letting people choose from
what, and how random." Results are to be auditable, which "is not the same
thing as putting a big block of unstyled text under a reading". Offered choices
for what makes a generated inference replayable, Mark chose: formula-derived
inferences recompute exactly, and model-generated ones are stored and plainly
labelled as generated.

**Where tensions and synergies come from (2026-09-23).** "Tarot, weather,
lunar/solar cycles, seasons, divination, to the extent that you can get facts,
references, corroboration, standards, any of which can be extrapolated from and
interrelated, that is the source of tensions and synergies. ... I do know that
localization can yield context for particular kinds of readings, and that's a
precise calculation with utility beyond divination. I do know that there is all
manner of prior art of formulaically extrapolating automatically readings from
conditions set by the reader, and that a systemization of these contextual
patterns can then rely on real data to transparently produce honestly generated
inferences like prompts, synergistic readings across many individual divinatory
elements/readings/user responses, such that reflective trends can be drawn."
From offered sources, Mark chose traditional methods, user-configurable methods,
and the person's own history, all together.

**The terms, and two axes (2026-09-23).** Mark: "Ambiance can be a term that
defines data in the background, ambient data. Then we have
background->foreground->focused/asserted? And moving things between those
states can be a permissioned, scripted, or otherwise automatic process too".
Offered a refinement that separates attention from keeping, Mark chose two
axes, **attention** (background → foreground → focus) and **keeping**. He named
the second after Isocosm's "three tiers of keeping", because TERMINOLOGY's
*Standing* already means community reputation. Offered options for what a move
records, he chose: every move between keeping levels records who or what made
it, the person or the rule, script or engine with its version.

**A reservoir is all of an identity's meres (2026-09-23).** On the word for a
data domain, Mark said "Reservoir, provided i'm not mangling terms of art".
Told that IBM defines a data reservoir for a whole organization, he chose that
scale: an identity's **reservoir** is its whole governed set of meres, and "data
domain" stays plain prose, one mere per domain.

**A mere is a data domain's repository, and Eidetic is its persistence
(2026-09-23).** Asked where examined memory lives, Mark answered: "Is eidetic not
mere's persistence layer? Like, i would think of a mere as a session, and i
would think the eidetic stack saves it and archives locally, and the mere can
pick those sessions and open them. A remote user would be able to access or
clone the mere depending on their identifier/credentials. And then the meres
are like a self-managed set of repositories for your data, synced, branching,
merging, and conflicts resolving going forward". On Eidetic: "look where
eidetic is. Why would it exist if not to do what you're acting like a mere
should do absent any dependency? Eidetic is that dependency. You are doing a
weird caveat where you're pigeon holing part of the whole, like trying to say
you walked to the grocery store without using your left leg. Don't make gaps."

**The questions that followed (2026-09-23).**
- **Keeping for generated inference.** Asked whether a stored model-generated
  inference is short-lived or kept: "I would suppose it depends on the model and
  the purpose. So think of this as two options".
- **Cross-app ambiance.** From offered options, Mark chose opt-in per mere and
  per app.
- **What an engine is.** "I would love for it to be the first [a pack or mod],
  but right now, every engine is like not that. They're all pretty integral to
  the app they each inhabit. I don't think any of them are scripts or wasm.
  They're all pretty much systems rust. So a new kind is more appropriate,
  living inside or outside the repo of a consumer."

**Keeping in Mere's own memory levels, and no new word for a session
(2026-09-23).** Earlier offers had used words of the assistant's own: "noted"
and "asserted" for keeping levels, and "checkout" for a working state. Mark was
then told that Mere's memory design (Alembic, 2026-06-09) already has three
levels with promotion and eviction, and that Mere already names a mere's live
working state a session. He chose, from offered options:
- **Keeping runs ambient → short-term → long-term → codicil.** The ambient tier
  is the one new level:
  - examining something puts it in short-term memory;
  - keeping it promotes it to long-term;
  - distilling it makes a codicil;
  - moves are recorded in the structural event log;
  - "noted" and "asserted" are retired as level names.
- **"Checkout" is retired.** A live working state of a mere is a session and an
  archived one is a codicil. Every other sense of *session* (projection,
  admitted, reading) is always qualified.

## 3. Examples

### Browsing: Turnstone

Mark's Wikipedia case is the plainest one. The article being read is in focus.
Every link on it that has not been followed is ambient. Following a link
captures the page into short-term memory, which is what Alembic's Recent holds
for networked content, and bookmarking promotes it to long-term. A link that
was examined but not followed is in short-term memory too; a link never
examined stays ambient. Turnstone is the browser "where browsing history is the
graph rather than a tab strip" ([TERMINOLOGY](../../TERMINOLOGY.md)). Mark:
"this obviously directly applies to turnstone."

### Practice: Woodshed

Mark's chord questions are the ambient tier of one chord in focus: the related
chords in the progression in focus, other progressions, the intervals from the
chord's root, the intervallic relations, and where each of those falls on the
neck. Each question is a different relation family, and each wants its own
scene.

Woodshed has built several of these
(`repos/woodshed/design_docs/2026-09-04_musical_projections_plan.md`, status
2026-09-08). Circle of fifths and Tonnetz are two musical readings over the
same keyed material; nearby candidates are ranked within each reading; an
anchored pitch-motion reading places material by distance from a captured
anchor; and a selected shape can be compared on the neck. Its ambient context
boundary is the stack's clearest statement of the idea: "available catalog
material, currently displayed material, inspected focus, and authored Card
occurrences are different states." §4 places those states on the two axes and
collects its rules.

### Simulation: Isocosm

The soup is the ambient tier of a world: what the simulation can generate at a
place from its basic facts. What is of note (a denizen, a location) persists
because something happened there; the rest regenerates on demand, and a thing
of note that stops mattering returns to the soup through garbage collection.
The sim plan names wilderness "the ambient tier for places, from which
candidate locations are derived and asserted when persisted"
(`repos/isometry/mesocosm/design_docs/2026-09-22_sim_plan.md`).

### Divination: Cleromancy

Mark's description of how a reading should explain itself:

> I'm running this computation on this data to derive a card from this deck
> and place it in this orientation in this position in this spread, the nth of
> m cards i've drawn so far. These positions in this spread have their purpose
> and meaning noted if i hover over them, and if I click on a card, I can see
> cards that are similar from this deck along the axes of meaning (suite,
> number, arcana, decks, other systems), and i can analyze any of the common
> and obvious and known divination methods this way… none of this is secret
> knowledge, it's just been difficult to analyze each as a system.

A drawn card is the focus. Its ambient tier holds:

- cards near it along each axis of meaning;
- the same card in other decks and other systems;
- each tradition's correspondences, which "don't need to be a click away";
- the meanings of the spread's positions;
- the other cards in the reading;
- the person's prior readings;
- the sky at the moment of the draw.

Mark on what to compare:

> readings, the opposite; they need contextualization relative to prior
> readings, the available cards/reverses/positionings, and interpretations. you
> want to be able to compare, individually, this card in this orientation in
> this position against variances in any of those, in a way that reveals
> relations, as opposed to a big blob of cards that can only be described in a
> gestalt, untractable fashion.

The dimensions stay typed:

> you would look at each individual part of the scope and analyze each in
> relation, arranging things as appropriate to determine, like, tensions and
> synergies and methods of analyzing the cards, position, orientation, deck,
> ordering, time of day, geographic location, astrological context... like,
> those are valuable typed and handled atomically, not blobbed together on a
> canvas

What feeds the ambient tier is bounded by two choices Mark made from offered
options:
- **Time and place.** Time is always recorded; place is opt-in.
- **Network sources such as weather.** Local-first holds: a source is used only
  when the person turns it on, and what it returns is stored with the reading.

The data domain is divination, journaling and RNG, with Knot, Redshank and
Isocosm as neighbors (§2).

### Writing: Knot

The Rosette entry in the
[Scenograph content catalog](../research/2026-08-18_scenograph_content_catalog.md)
describes Knot as a creative-writing analysis scene over a document, where
"derived analysis edges arrive as revealed cells and crystallize only when the
writer keeps them". Revealed cells are the ambient tier of a text. Keeping one
is a promotion.

## 4. Two axes: attention and keeping

Woodshed's ambient context boundary names four states: "available catalog
material, currently displayed material, inspected focus, and authored Card
occurrences are different states." The Isocosm record reads them as one ladder:
"what the engine can generate at this address, what is realised in the scene,
what is foregrounded, and what is asserted". The 2026-09-23 rulings split that
ladder into two independent axes, and place the second one on Mere's existing
memory levels (§2).

### Attention

Attention runs **background → foreground → focus**: what is not shown, what is
shown, and what is being inspected. It is curation in the swatch design's
sense: per instance and transient, and it never syncs. You move along it by
navigating.

### Keeping

Keeping runs **ambient → short-term → long-term → codicil**. The three upper
levels are the Alembic memory design's
([Alembic memory and codicils](../technical_architecture/2026-06-09_alembic_memory_and_engrams.md)
§2, written when codicils were called engrams). The ambient tier is the level
below them.

- **Ambient.** Generated by an engine and regenerated on demand. Never stored,
  and never in conflict.
- **Short-term** (Alembic's *Recent*). What was examined or captured: "The
  immediate session; evicted by default". Eviction "is the default and is
  configurable", and "the default is a user-overridable policy, never a silent
  one". This is the collectible memory ruled on 2026-09-23. Where collection
  needs criteria, D11's roots and reachability give their shape.
- **Long-term** (*Saved*). "**Promotion** short to long is an affirmative act.
  **Tagging or bookmarking a node promotes it**". Durable across sessions.
- **Codicil.** Distilled from short- or long-term memory: immutable,
  content-addressed and shareable. "Engrams are immutable, so they are never
  mutated or demoted. They can only be **deleted** outright." TERMINOLOGY
  records that *Codicil* "replaces **Engram**".

Isocosm names the same axis in a world's terms, its "three tiers of keeping":
- **ambient**, the soup;
- **of note**, which is literally a note on a thing (ruling 80: "the sim just
  makes notes in the way a player would too") and can lapse;
- **legendary**, which is never lossy.

The two axes are independent. A long-term reading from last year sits in the
background. A candidate chord under inspection is in focus and still ambient.

**Moves.** Moves between keeping levels can be explicit, and Mark: "a
permissioned, scripted, or otherwise automatic process too". Every move records
who or what made it: the person, or the rule, script or engine with its
version. The record is the structural event log, which Alembic defines as "an
append-only log of graph mutations (node spawned, node removed, edge asserted /
retracted, node moved), with periodic `GraphSnapshot` checkpoints", and which
also carries undo and the Timeline. The moves:
- **examining** an ambient item puts it in short-term memory, automatically;
- **keeping** promotes it to long-term, by the person's act or by a permitted
  rule or script;
- **distilling** makes a codicil, by the person or by an Athanor proposal the
  memory authority applies;
- **forgetting** evicts short-term memory under its policy; Athanor is "the only
  place blobs are dropped";
- **demotion and deletion** of short- and long-term memory are "free at will";
  a codicil can only be deleted.

**Woodshed's states on the two axes.** *Analysis*; the mapping is not ruled.

| Woodshed's term | Attention | Keeping |
|---|---|---|
| available catalog material | background | ambient |
| currently displayed material | foreground | any |
| inspected focus | focus | any |
| authored Card occurrences | any | long-term |
| discovery history, remembered coordinates | any | short-term |

The last row is Woodshed's instruction to "Separate discovery history/remembered
coordinates from current membership".

**Rules the precedents already carry:**

- **Focus never promotes.** Woodshed: "Focus never adds a Card or auditions it.
  Hear and Add remain explicit." Mere's
  [subgraph derivation](2026-06-13_subgraph_derivation_from_selection.md) makes
  the same rule on the graph canvas: "Steps 1-4 are pure read-side projection
  over graph truth. Nothing is committed while you explore", and "Only
  crystallize writes". Athanor keeps it too: "Athanor does not directly mutate
  graph truth". Examining an item puts it in short-term memory; it does not
  promote it.
- **Every ambient item explains itself.** Woodshed: "Every suggestion carries a
  reason and its reading."
- **Scores stay distinct.** Woodshed keeps "Fifth distance, P/L/R depth, and
  summed pitch motion" as "distinct columns; do not blend them into an
  unexplained relevance number", and requires that "unavailable scores are
  distinct from zero".
- **Breadth is the person's.** "Context visibility and breadth are user
  preferences."
- **Nothing is evicted silently.** At capacity, Woodshed offers "to replace
  quiet, unpinned background", must "protect authored, focused, and pinned
  material, preserve coordinates when material returns, and expose breadth
  limits", and must not "silently evict Cards to admit suggestions".
- **A scene carries domain meaning.** "Existing Grid/Snake/Circle names describe
  geometry; they do not establish musical meaning such as a circle of fifths."

## 5. Sessions, codicils and Eidetic

Mere's founding thesis is session-shaped. Pandect's crate documentation quotes
it: "**Mere multiplexes durable graph sessions. Engines are replaceable content
producers. Hosts are attach clients. Graph truth is never browser profile
state.**" It describes itself as "everything a Mere session gathers under one
cover": session manifests, graph and view sidecars, worker declarations,
content and facet stores, settings, and the persona wallet.

The lexicon gives the tier-1 mere as "Backed by `eidetic`". Eidetic freezes and
thaws sessions (`crates/system/pandect/src/graph_codicil.rs`):
- "Save as graph codicil" freezes a live graph into an immutable,
  content-addressed Eidetic codicil;
- "Open as session" thaws one back;
- browsing a codicil is read-only, and "editing forks a thaw";
- codicil compose merges snapshots and records their lineage.

That is the repository Mark describes: sessions saved and archived by Eidetic,
reopened from the archive, forking and merging.

The [Alembic implementation plan](../implementation_strategy/2026-06-24_alembic_implementation_plan.md)
records how far it is built: "slices A-C landed; merge, promotion, event-log,
LoRA, and settings/Timeline follow-ons remain deferred."

## 6. Analysis: ambiance is a third plane

*Not ruled, except where a ruling is cited.*

The [swatch primitive design](2026-06-27_swatch_primitive_design.md) §2 splits
state into two planes: **truth**, "Changed only by assert / retract / delete",
and **curation** ("scope, arrangement, lens, which cells are hidden, camera,
selection, mode"), which is "Private to each instance". The ambient tier fits
neither. It is not truth, because nothing stored it. It is not curation,
because an engine produced it rather than a view choice. Curation acts *on*
ambiance: breadth, visibility and the choice of scene change what is shown
without changing what the engine generates. The model therefore has three
planes: truth, ambiance and curation.

The two axes land on those planes:
- attention is curation;
- on the keeping axis, the ambient tier is the ambiance plane, and short-term,
  long-term and codicils are truth held as memory, all persisted by Eidetic, the
  mere's persistence (ruled).

The discipline is not new. Athanor already emits "distillation candidates, GC
proposals, facet candidates, and engram manifests for the host / memory
authority to apply". Eidetic's R0 rule already says "derived views ("recent",
caches, indices) are projections over the one engram store, never a second
authority". The ambient tier applies the same discipline to domain engines.

Consequences:

1. **Ambiance never conflicts and never syncs as data.** The Isocosm record says
   so for its world: "the ambient tier is regenerated from the merged facts and
   can never conflict (rulings 69 and 70); only asserted facts and notes can".
   What travels is stored memory and codicils, plus the identity and version of
   the engines, which live in a registry (ruled, §9).
2. **Replay means regeneration.** Given the same stored facts and the same
   engine versions, the same ambiance comes back. Every ambient item names its
   engine and version alongside its reason. Every move records the engine and
   version that made it (ruled), so a promotion pins the version, and a later
   engine change cannot silently rewrite what someone kept.
3. **Model-generated inference is stored when produced (ruled).** It cannot be
   regenerated byte for byte, so it cannot rely on regeneration. It is stored
   with its inputs, model identity and output, and labelled as generated. Which
   keeping level it enters depends on the model and the purpose (ruled).
4. **Localization makes ambiance a query.** A focus, an engine and a budget
   produce related material, each item with its reasons. Changing the focus
   changes the neighborhood; it does not change the truth.
5. **Ambiance can cross meres, by opt-in per mere and per app (ruled).** Any
   app can open any of an identity's meres on purpose. A mere's material
   appears in another app's ambient tier only once the person has opted that
   mere in for that app. Where it is opted in, an engine can relate a focus in
   one mere to material in another: a reading's prompt to a Knot note, a derived
   seed to an Isocosm world. That is the dataspace's "relationships added
   pay-as-you-go" ([TERMINOLOGY](../../TERMINOLOGY.md)) appearing first as
   ambiance. A relation becomes a link only when it is kept.
6. **Sharing crosses as codicils.** Alembic's boundary is "local-until-engram
   is the privacy boundary (only engrams can leave the persona's scope)". A
   remote user's clone (§2) therefore travels as codicils and opens on the other
   side as a session.
7. **One neighborhood, several scenes.** Ambiance is data, not a picture, so
   one neighborhood can be realised by several scenes. This is already a
   platform rule: one source may have several projected instances
   ([projection scenes](../../2026-08-23_projection_scenes_and_graph_native_platform.md)
   §4). Force-directed is the default, per Mark. A domain scene has to carry
   domain meaning, per Woodshed's rule in §4.

## 7. Naming

- **"tier".** Mere's in-product vocabulary already uses tiers for socialization:
  mere t1, moot t2, moothold t3, gemot t4 ([DOC_README](../../DOC_README.md),
  working principles). The
  [physics scenes plan](../implementation_strategy/2026-06-22_physics_scenes_and_tangibility_plan.md)
  uses "Tier 2, ambient separate sims" for the living backdrop. *The ambient
  tier* is neither: it is the bottom level of the keeping axis.
- **"ambient".** The
  [meaningful physics signals plan](../implementation_strategy/2026-06-24_meaningful_physics_signals_plan.md)
  has an ambient pulse, in which "the backdrop sims read the system's live
  state", and the physics scenes plan's living backdrop is "ambient, cheap,
  default-intangible". Those are the system's ambiance, drawn from runtime
  state. The ambient tier is the domain's ambiance, drawn from domain engines.
  The Isocosm record already set the two side by side on 2026-09-20.
- **"keeping", not "standing".** TERMINOLOGY's *Standing* is "community-scoped
  reputation derived from signed receipts of commitment follow-through". The
  axis takes Isocosm's word instead (ruled, §2).
- **Alembic's level names.** The keeping levels above the ambient tier are
  Alembic's short-term, long-term and codicil, shown in the interface as Recent
  and Saved. The assistant's earlier "noted" and "asserted" are retired (ruled,
  §2).
- **"session", not "checkout".** A mere's live working state is a session, the
  durable graph session of Mere's multiplexer thesis. The earlier "checkout" is
  retired (ruled, §2). The other senses of the word are always qualified:
  projection session, admitted session, the Session subgraph binding,
  Cleromancy's reading session, and the per-persona web session store of
  cookies and storage
  ([native session store plan](../implementation_strategy/2026-06-23_native_session_store_plan.md)).
- **"reservoir".** IBM's data reservoir is "a reference architecture that
  balances the desire for easy access to data with information governance and
  security" (Redbook SG24-8274 abstract). It is one governed ecosystem for a
  whole organization. The ruling adopts that scale: an identity's reservoir is
  its whole governed set of meres. Until 2026-09-23 TERMINOLOGY called
  reservoir "shorthand for explaining the distinction, not a minted term". It
  is now minted. It is unrelated to *reservoir sampling*, the random-sampling
  algorithm, which matters in RNG work. *Vault*, the other word offered, stays
  with the personae vault, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\personae\vault`

## 9. Rulings on the questions this document raised

All ruled 2026-09-23. Each was a choice among offered options, except where §2
quotes Mark's own answer.

| Question | Ruling |
|---|---|
| What does exploring leave behind, outside the sim? | Short-term memory. What was examined, and where it sat, is remembered and evicted under its policy. Where collection needs criteria, they follow roots and reachability. |
| Model-generated inference | Stored when produced, with its inputs, model identity and output, and labelled as generated. The keeping level it enters depends on the model and the purpose. |
| Cross-mere ambiance | Opt-in, per mere and per app. |
| Where engines are declared | A registry of versioned engines, with each mere naming its domain's defaults and apps and people able to add others. For now an engine is a new kind of component, systems Rust living inside or outside a consumer's repository. Pack or mod is the aspiration. |
| Where examined memory lives | In the session's short-term memory. Eidetic persists it as the mere's persistence. |
| State model | Two axes. Attention runs background → foreground → focus. Keeping runs ambient → short-term → long-term → codicil. Every move records who or what made it, in the structural event log. |
| Terms | *Ambiance* and *reservoir* (an identity's whole set of meres, at IBM's scale) are added to TERMINOLOGY. A working state is a session. "Noted", "asserted" and "checkout" are retired. |
| Reconciliation | Annotate the family composition thesis, and rewrite TERMINOLOGY's *mere* entry per data domain. |

No design question is left open here. The shape of the engine registry is work
for the plan that builds it. The
[reservoir plan](../implementation_strategy/2026-09-23_reservoir_plan.md)
implements the per-domain meres, their sessions and archive, and the access and
ambient grants. Alembic's own open decisions stand, including the
event log's shape (its §10, item 5).
